Malaria diagnosis in resource-limited settings still depends on a trained microscopist examining blood smears by eye. This project automates that step: given a microscopic image, a YOLOv11 instance segmentation model localizes every parasite at pixel level and classifies its species and life stage in a single pass, feeding a real-time inference GUI built for clinical review.

The hard part was not the model. It was the data: public malaria datasets disagree on everything, from annotation format (binary masks, COCO-style JSON, plain-text circular and rectangular boxes) to class definitions and image quality. The pipeline normalizes all of them into one YOLO segmentation corpus, with negative samples of healthy cells to control false positives.

Pipeline

  1. Debug analysis. Audit every source dataset: formats, class definitions, annotation quality, corrupted or incomplete labels.
  2. Data processing. Convert masks, COCO JSON and TXT coordinates into one YOLO segmentation format, with smart class mapping and background-cell filtering (WBC, RBC, debris).
  3. Training. YOLOv11-seg with incremental learning across datasets, checkpoint saving and recovery, so the corpus can grow without full retraining.
  4. Evaluation & visualization. mAP50 and mAP50-95, per-class precision and recall, segmentation overlays for clinical interpretation.

Design decisions

Flat 17 classes, not species-then-stage

A hierarchical design (first classify the species, then the life stage) looks cleaner on paper. It was rejected because early-stage parasites look alike across species: the hierarchy would discard exactly the distinguishing signal the model needs. The flat 17-class head lets the network learn species and stage jointly.

Report the honest number

62% mAP50 is measured on the full heterogeneous test corpus, spanning all source datasets and their quality range, not on a narrower favourable slice. On multi-class medical segmentation with this data diversity, the honest number is the one worth reporting.

Scope note. This is a research and screening-support system, not a certified diagnostic device. Training ran on Google Colab; the award refers to the AWS University Engagement Program competition.
